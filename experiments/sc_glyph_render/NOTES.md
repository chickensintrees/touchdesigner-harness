# sc_glyph_render: text made of text

## Intent
A picture re-drawn entirely out of the glyphs of one font (the pack's `chicago`) in strict 1-bit black and white. It works when the picture reads twice: from across the room it is a marble bust, a corridor, a wallpaper; with your nose on the screen every cell is a letter and the letters run on as the pack's sentences. Brightness never picks the letter; it only picks how the letter is inked. The music must be visible: the text marches on the beat, the snare inverts the frame, the kick thins and swells.

Since 2026-11-10 evening the scene has **eight takes** (below). The cutter returns to a scene many times in a track, and the first cut showed the same picture every time: a model over one huge grey word. Each return is now a different composition, scale, subject or mode of the technique, and `Seed` changes model, phrase, side, camera roll, tile count and cell size inside a take.

## Words and models (2026-11-11)
Everything the scene shows that is content sits in `DEFAULTS` at the top of `build.py`: `MODELS` (name, scale, yaw per model), `PHRASES` (the running text), `POSTERS` (the big words), and `FACE`, `FACE_NOTE`, `FACE_SIZE` (what the Describe texts call the font, and how large it is drawn in its cell). `build()` reads them once through `LIB['scene_words'](scene, DEFAULTS)`, so a pack's `scenes/sc_glyph_render/words.json` replaces any of them by name. The defaults are generic: six objects of the shipped pack (marble_bust, space_helmet, gramophone, rubber_duck, hourglass, amphora) and plain sentences about real-time pictures and a catalogue of unexplained objects. `check_words.py` asserts that every default phrase and poster word uses only characters the atlases hold.

Arrival, first half second (`shot['age']`): the page is typed in over 0.3 to 0.5 s and until then the frame is the bare drawing (rows from the top in 0 and 7, random cells in 1, 2 and 6, columns from the left in 3, centre out in 4); the model lands from 1.25x to 1.8x its size; the corridor lunges forward; the quadtree starts as all 96 px blocks and splits; the wallpaper starts as one tile and zooms out to its grid.

## Describe and Input (2026-11-11, for the website)

With `Input` set as well, the running text of every take is the one in `DESCRIBE_INPUT` ("THIS IS ANOTHER SCENE'S PICTURE, REDRAWN AS TYPE. ..."), because the take's own sentences talk about a 3D model that is then not there. The poster words stay the take's own.

**Input.** The picture enters at the model render, so the switch (`src`) sits between `render` and `tone`. `insel` (Select TOP) reads the TOP named by `Input`; `inprep` (`shaders/inprep.frag`) makes it look like a render: luminance to the power 2 (mid grey lands near the black/white line), alpha 0 where the picture is darker than about 0.1, so dark areas become the void and the picture gets a silhouette, a keyline and the white rim. It is also zoomed per take (`inz`: macro 2.6x, wall 1.3x, quadtree 1.25x), rolled with the take's camera roll, lands from larger at the cut and swells on the kick, as the model does. `inp['on']` is 1 only for a TOP outside this COMP, so the scene cannot read its own `out1`; a wrong path or a non-TOP is ignored without a warning. While Input is on the 3D render does not cook.

All eight takes use it (`captures/in_sheet.png`, test picture, seed 0.37):
- 1 wall: the best. The tickers stay readable and the picture is the same wall in inverse video.
- 6 wallpaper, 5 quadtree, 0 poster: strong. Mirrored negative tiles of the picture; letter size following the picture's contrast; the picture as lit page and shadow with outlines.
- 4 corridor: the end wall moves nearer (k 0.55, was 0.30) and is a screen showing the picture; every door is the picture too. The end-wall word is dropped.
- 7 keyhole: the picture is seen through the giant word and its outlines carry on outside. Reads more as outline than picture.
- 3 page: the picture as a negative print on the page. 2 macro: 2.6x into the picture with fat pixels, abstract by design.

## Assets
Nothing in this folder names a folder on a machine: `build.py` finds the repository from `td.project.folder`, and every file parameter is an expression on `mod.paths` (`experiments/sw_hub/paths_module.py`). The scene uses three kinds of file, and builds without the first two:

| Uses | From | With no assets |
|---|---|---|
| The font called `chicago`, for the six atlases and the poster word (the shipped pack answers with IBM Plex Mono Medium, drawn at `FACE_SIZE` 1.2 cell widths) | `mod.paths.font('chicago')` | TouchDesigner's default face (Verdana on this machine). `build.py` then picks, per atlas, the largest font size whose widest character fits the cell (44, 33, 22, 17, 13, 11 px for the 48 to 12 px cells), so glyphs never spill into the next cell. Atlases, poster sizes and the code strip are all made at build time from whatever font the Text TOPs get, so nothing precomputed is stored. |
| Six models and their base colour images | `<assets>/models/<slug>/model.obj`, `basecolor.png` via `mod.paths.model_file`, `mod.paths.model_texture` | Every model is TouchDesigner's bundled teapot. `build.py` reads the OBJ's bounds and adds a Transform POP (`m<i>/fit`) that centres it and makes it 1.3 units long, and turns each of the six a further 60 degrees so the page take's three are not identical. The texture is `data/standin.png`. |
| Shaders, stand-in texture | `shaders/*.frag`, `data/standin.png` (64 x 64 grey bands, 170 bytes, committed) via `mod.paths.here(...)` | always present |

To see it: set the COMP's `Assets` parameter to an empty folder and rebuild from `build.py`; blank it and rebuild to return. Captures with no assets: `captures/p_none_sheet.png` (all takes, seed 0.37), `captures/p_none_modes_sheet.png` (Describe on take 1, Input on take 5, both on take 0, and take 0 two seconds apart). The same with assets after the conversion: `captures/p_with_sheet.png`, `captures/p_with_modes_sheet.png`; before it: `captures/p_before_sheet.png`.

## Inputs / outputs
- Inputs: the hub feed (`hub`), `shot` (take, seed, age), six library models (`MODELS` in `DEFAULTS`; all loaded at build time and switched by render flags), the pack's `chicago` font (see Assets for what replaces them when absent).
- Output TOP: `out1`, 1280x720, only #000 and #FFF (asserted by `check_1bit.py`).
- Cost: 0.93 to 1.44 ms per frame summed over the operators that cook each frame, measured once per take in two passes (budget 8 ms). One reading of 9.9 ms (take 0, first pass) had `tone` and `glyph` at 3.8 ms CPU each, which is the contention spike TRAPS.md describes; the second pass read 1.09 ms for the same take.
- Switching take loads nothing: everything is parameters and uniforms, and setting `Take` and capturing in the same call already shows the new take.

## Technique
`build.py` builds everything; GLSL is in `shaders/`.

1. `tk` (Constant CHOP): every number of the current `TAKES` row as a channel. `tkd`: values derived from take + seed + feed (side, which model, cell size, tiles, reveal).
2. `render` (1280x720): the models as Geometry COMPs; `tkd['base']` picks one, `tk['multi']` shows its neighbours too. Key + rim + weak camera fill. Camera distance, height, pitch and roll come from the take.
3. `poster` (Text TOP, the pack's font, 1280x720): one of `POSTERS`, sized per phrase from `evalTextSize`.
4. `tone.frag` (1280x720, rgba16float): r = luminance, g = poster mask (scaled and moved per take), b = model alpha dilated by 14 texels (keyline), a = rim + 2 x tile parity. Its mode decides what the luminance is: lit model over the void (0), flat inverse silhouette on a wall (1), an analytic corridor (2), a lit page with the model as a hole (3). It also tiles the render for the wallpaper.
5. `atlas0..5`: six Text TOPs, each a 16x4 grid of ASCII 32..95 rasterised at exactly one cell size (48x64 down to 12x16). `atlas` (Script TOP) stacks them into one 768x816 texture once, so the shader can use any size, and several in one frame. `build.py` refuses to build if `glyph.frag`'s `ATL` table does not match `CELLS`.
6. `codes` (Script TOP): the running texts as a 553x10 float texture, one texel per character, one row per text (row 0 the pack's phrases, rows 1 to 8 the takes' Describe texts, row 9 the Describe text for Input).
7. `glyph.frag` (1280x720): for each pixel, find its cell (fixed grid, or the quadtree descent: 16 luminance taps per block, split while max - min exceeds the threshold), read the character for that cell, fetch the atlas texel 1:1 (or 2x / 3x for fat pixels), then ink it:
   - per pixel: luminance > 0.5 (with a 4x4 Bayer band) means inverse video, so silhouettes are crisp even when the cells are huge;
   - per cell: the weight. Dark side: nothing, greyed (50% stipple), regular, bold (smear right). Lit side: bold black, regular; greyed and bare white only while a kick lifts luminance past 1;
   - a dark pixel in a mostly lit cell is plain black with no letter through it, and the reverse;
   - the poster word is cut per pixel: grey block with bold white type at rest, white block with black type on a kick or beat; or (keyhole) the word is the only place the picture exists;
   - cells not yet typed after a cut or a section change carry no letters: the bare 1-bit drawing.

## Channel to visual
| Feed | What you see |
|---|---|
| `shot.take`, `shot.seed`, `shot.age` | which take; model, phrase, side, roll, tiles, fat-pixel size, vanishing point; the arrival |
| `energy`, sampled once per bar | cell size, inside the take's own range (take 0: 36 to 12 px; 3: 18 to 12; 4: 18 or 15; 7: 15 or 12; fixed in the others) |
| `beat_pos` | the text advances two characters per beat (tickers in the wall take: 2, 4 or 6 by row); the model leans; the corridor travels half a tube per beat |
| `beat_i` | wallpaper: the checker of tiles swaps polarity each beat |
| `evolve` | model swing, tilt, bob and drift; void bands; corridor roll; wallpaper slide |
| `bar_pos` | model roll |
| `section` | which model, which side, which poster phrase, where the text starts (all offset by seed) |
| `section_pos` | first 8%: the page is retyped; first 10%: the model whips once round |
| `hit_mid` > 0.45 (snare) | the whole frame goes inverse video for the length of the hit (take 3, already inverse, goes black) |
| `hit_low` (kick) | model tips back and swells 7%, its lit side thins, the void's type gets heavier, the poster snaps white, the corridor's tubes surge, the quadtree splits more blocks, the keyhole's void fills with greyed type |
| `pulse` > 0.6 | the poster / keyhole flash, a short blink on each beat |
| `hit_high` (hat) | stray characters replace up to about 13% of the letters |

Seen following the feed, in `captures/m_sheet.png` (each take captured twice, about 11 s apart; feed read in the same frame): take 0 with hit_mid 0.51 is inverse, without it is not; take 1 with hit_mid 0.56 is a white wall; take 3 with hit_mid 0.94 is black, without it white; take 4 with hit_mid 0.46 is inverse; take 6 at beat 265 and beat 288 has opposite checkers; take 7 on a kick (`take_7.png`, hit_low 0.71, and the first two frames of `s_sheet.png`, hit_low 0.28) shows greyed type across the void; off the kick the void is black (`arrive_sheet.png`, hit_low 0.0, taken one build earlier with the model at 1.9x).

## Weak, honestly
- With no assets: six teapots are one subject, so "which model" (section, seed) changes nothing but the angle, and the page take is three teapots in a row. The default face has one-pixel strokes at 11 and 13 px and is thinner and greyer in the mass than Chicago; it is readable but it is not a bitmap font, and the 2x and 3x fat pixels of the macro take show its ragged thresholded edges. The Describe sentences then still call the font by the name in `FACE` and say "bitmap letters". The teapot's lid and spout are small features, so the wall take (2.5x) reads as a white blob with a handle more than as a teapot.
- Describe: the sentences are running text cut by the picture, so no take can be read start to finish in a still. Best to worst: wall (1) and page (3) read almost anywhere; wallpaper (6) and keyhole (7) in runs of a line or two; poster (0) is greyed 12 px stipple in the void and reads properly only on the model and where the bands pass; corridor (4) walls are nearly bare so the sentences come through on the floor, doors and ceiling only; quadtree (5) reads along its rows of same-size letters ("FLAT AREAS KEEP HUGE LETTERS, EDGES GET TINY ONES.") and is fragments elsewhere; macro (2) at 3x shows about 60 letters and the lit side of the model swallows some (`desc_s_2.png`: "MACRO: / FONT P / S DRAW / 3 SCRE").
- Describe: the poster word still sits behind or under the model, so it is often half hidden ("ADE OF / LETTERS", "RAWN / TYPE"; the page header "THE FRAME / ..VERTED" under a model). Same as the pack's own words.
- Describe: only upper case, digits and punctuation exist in the atlases. No live values are shown: the text is baked.
- Input: the picture is reduced to three tones (void, white type on black, black type on white) cut at luminance of about 0.1 and 0.45. A mid-grey, low-contrast input becomes one flat tone; a full-bright input has no void and so no rim. Soft gradients turn into wandering thin outlines (the rim follows the 0.1 contour), which is lively on the test picture and may be noise on a busier scene.
- Input: only seen on the test picture, never on another scene's output, and never in motion.
- Input: a Select TOP pulls the other scene's `out1`, which makes that TOP cook if nothing else is cooking it.
- Still never seen in motion. Every judgement, in all three passes, is from stills: the arrivals, the ticker rows, the beat-swapping wallpaper checker and the corridor travel have not been watched at speed. The wallpaper checker swap (half the frame flips every 0.48 s) and the snare inversion are the first things to calm down if it strobes; the swap is `uMod.w` in `build.py`.
- Takes 0 and 7 both put one large word across the frame (never a wordmark, and 7 is cropped and off-centre). They are the two nearest in idea.
- Take 3 (page) is the weakest picture: the void bands leave the white page patchy rather than a clean printed form, and the header word is cut by the top edge for three-line phrases.
- Take 1 depends on the model: a boxy or round one at 2.5x fills the frame, so the "wall" becomes a white page with a few dark edges (the helmet in `gen_t1.png`; the hourglass is two posts and a white X, `g3_t1_s05.png`); a figure is a clear ghost (the bust in `g3_t1_s50.png`).
- Take 5 with a boxy model at 1.9x is a busy mosaic where the object is hard to name; with a face (the bust) it is the best frame of the scene (`hero.png`).
- Take 2 at 3x has about 12 x 5 letters, so when the page is half typed it is mostly an abstract dither with a few letters.
- The model and the poster phrase still change at a section boundary inside a shot (with a whip and a retype). That was a feature when the scene had one look; with 2 to 9 s shots it may read as a second cut.

- Greyed (stippled) text is hard to read at 12 px; it is doing tonal work there, not reading work.
